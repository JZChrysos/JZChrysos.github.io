	\maketitle 
	
	Let $V$ be a set of size $n<\infty$, and let $f:V\to V$ be some function with no fixed points. One can form a simple graph $G$ whose vertex set is $V$ by saying that 
	$$
	v\sim w \; \iff \; f(v) = w \;\; \text{or} \;\; f(w)=v.
	$$
	Let's explore some properties of graphs like this.\\
	
	\noindent \textbf{Problem}: What are the least and greatest numbers of edges that $G$ can have, in terms of $n$?\\
	
	\noindent \textbf{Problem}: Show that $G$ does not contain a subgraph isomorphic to $K_4$.\\
	
	\noindent \textbf{Problem}: Show that $G$ is 3-colorable by the following steps:
	\begin{enumerate}[(i)]
		\item Show there is an \textit{independent} set $U\subseteq V$ for which every $v\in G$ has $f^j(v)\in U$ for some $j$.
		\item Show that $G$ has a 3-coloring.
	\end{enumerate}
